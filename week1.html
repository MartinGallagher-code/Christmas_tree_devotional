Week One — First Week of Advent

The Tree

"He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree, so that we might die to sins and live for righteousness; by his wounds you have been healed." 1 Peter 2:24 (ESV)
Week 1 of 4

Preparation This Week

Find a suitable Christmas tree — preferably a live one — and place it prominently in your home. This week, the tree stands alone: no lights, no ornaments, no decorations. The bare tree is the point. Gather your family around it for worship using the readings below.

Why a Christmas Tree?

When I was a child, after my family had picked out our Christmas tree and my father had brought it into our house, my family would spend about an hour decorating it. Then we would all go off to bed or back to our playing. The tree was simply a mantle for displaying heirlooms of unknown origin that came out again each December. It never occurred to me to wonder: Why a tree?

From the first chapters of Genesis through to the last chapter of Revelation, God uses trees to tell His story. He created man in His image to commune with Him through a life of worship. The eternal life offered through the Tree of Life was lost through the first Adam, and regained through the second Adam, Jesus Christ.

The center of God's story is the tree at Calvary, on which Jesus the Christ was destined to die. This week, use your Christmas tree to help you reflect on your place in God's story.

The Story Behind the Tree

In the beginning, God planted a garden and placed within it two trees of supreme significance: the Tree of Life, offering communion and eternal fellowship with God, and the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil, representing the boundary of creaturely obedience. Adam and Eve were invited to eat freely — except from one tree. They ate. The fellowship was broken, the garden was closed, and the Tree of Life was barred to them.

But God was not finished with trees. He would use them again and again throughout His Word — as symbols of strength and growth, as places of curse and redemption, as images of the coming kingdom. And at the center of human history, God would place His own Son on a tree, bearing the curse that Adam's tree had earned, so that the way to the Tree of Life might be opened once again.

The Christmas tree standing in your home is not a pagan relic or a meaningless decoration. It is an opportunity to tell that story — every year, in a form that your children can see, touch, and remember.

The Thread of the Tree

From the first page of Scripture to the last, God uses trees to tell His story. The readings below trace that thread — from the two trees in Eden, through the curse of the Law, into the wisdom literature's vision of the flourishing life, through the prophets' warnings and promises, to the cross where Jesus bore our sins "on the tree," and finally to the Tree of Life restored in the new creation. As you read, notice how what was lost in the garden is recovered at Calvary and completed in the city of God.

The Thread of the Tree: Genesis to Revelation

Creation & Fall

  • Genesis 2:4–17 God plants a garden and places two trees at its center — the Tree of Life and the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil
  • Genesis 3:1–24 The forbidden tree, the fall, and exile from the Tree of Life — the garden is closed

Curse & Law

  • Deuteronomy 21:22–23 "A hanged man is cursed by God" — the law that would one day point to Calvary

Wisdom & Flourishing

  • Psalm 1:1–6 The righteous like a tree planted by streams of water — the flourishing life God always intended
  • Proverbs 3:13–20 Wisdom "is a tree of life to those who lay hold of her" — blessing flows from the fear of the Lord
  • Proverbs 11:28–31 "The fruit of the righteous is a tree of life" — the one who wins souls is wise

Judgment & Promise

  • Isaiah 61:1–6 God's people called "oaks of righteousness, a planting of the Lord" — beauty for ashes
  • Ezekiel 17:22–24 God plants a tender sprig on a high mountain — all the trees of the field will know He is Lord
  • Daniel 4:10–26 Nebuchadnezzar's dream of the great tree cut down — God humbles the proud

Prophetic Imagery

  • Jeremiah 17:5–10 "Blessed is the man who trusts in the Lord... He is like a tree planted by water" — two destinies contrasted
  • Zechariah 11:1–6 The cedars and oaks fall — judgment sweeps through the proud trees of the nations

The Cross

  • Acts 5:27–33 "The God of our fathers raised Jesus, whom you killed by hanging him on a tree"
  • Acts 10:34–43 "They put him to death by hanging him on a tree" — and the promise of forgiveness through His name
  • Galatians 3:10–14 "Cursed is everyone who is hanged on a tree" — Christ bears the curse so that blessing might come
  • 1 Peter 2:21–25 "He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree" — the Shepherd who brings us home

Fruit & Discipleship

  • Matthew 7:15–23 "Every good tree bears good fruit" — you will recognize them by their fruit
  • Matthew 12:33–37 "Either make the tree good and its fruit good, or make the tree bad" — the heart revealed
  • Luke 13:6–9 The parable of the barren fig tree — patience, judgment, and one more year of grace
  • John 15:1–8 "I am the vine, you are the branches" — abiding in Christ, the living wood

Restoration

  • Revelation 2:1–7 "To the one who conquers I will grant to eat of the tree of life" — the promise renewed
  • Revelation 22:1–5 The Tree of Life restored, bearing twelve kinds of fruit, its leaves for the healing of the nations
