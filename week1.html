Week One — First Week of Advent

The Tree

"He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree, so that we might die to sins and live for righteousness; by his wounds you have been healed." 1 Peter 2:24 (ESV)
Week 1 of 4

Preparation This Week

Find a Christmas tree — a real one if you can — and set it up in your home. This week, the tree stands alone: no lights, no ornaments, no decorations. Leave it bare. That is the point. Gather your family around it for worship using the readings below.

Why a Tree?

Every December, families carry a tree into their homes and cover it with lights and ornaments. Most of us have done it for as long as we can remember. But have you ever stopped to ask: Why a tree?

The answer runs deeper than tradition. From the opening chapters of Genesis to the final pages of Revelation, God uses trees to tell the most important story ever told. A tree stood at the centre of Eden. A tree stood at the centre of Calvary. And a tree stands in the centre of the new creation, bearing fruit for the healing of the nations. The Bible begins and ends with a tree — and the whole gospel hangs between them.

The Garden and the Cross

In the beginning, God planted a garden and placed two trees at its heart. The Tree of Life offered eternal fellowship with God — communion without end. The Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil stood as a boundary: one command, one test of trust. Adam and Eve were free to eat from every tree in the garden — except that one.

They ate. And in that moment, everything changed. The fellowship was shattered, the garden was closed, and an angel with a flaming sword was set to guard the way to the Tree of Life. Humanity was exiled — not just from a place, but from the presence of God.

But God was not finished with trees.

Throughout the Old Testament, trees appear again and again — as images of the righteous life, as warnings of judgment, as signs of the coming kingdom. The Psalms picture the godly person as "a tree planted by streams of water." The prophets describe God's people as "oaks of righteousness." And woven through the Law is a strange and haunting declaration: "Cursed is everyone who is hanged on a tree."

That curse finds its answer at Calvary. The apostle Peter, standing before the rulers who crucified Jesus, put it plainly: "The God of our fathers raised Jesus, whom you killed by hanging him on a tree." Paul saw the same thread: Christ became a curse for us, hanging on a tree, so that the blessing of Abraham might come to all who believe. What Adam lost at one tree, Christ recovered at another.

And the story does not end at the cross. In the last chapter of the Bible, the Tree of Life reappears — no longer guarded, no longer forbidden. It stands beside the river of life, bearing twelve kinds of fruit, its leaves "for the healing of the nations." The way back to the tree is open. The exile is over.

Your Tree Tells This Story

The Christmas tree standing bare in your living room is not a meaningless decoration. It is not a leftover from paganism. It is an invitation — an opportunity to tell God's story to your family, year after year, in a form your children can see, touch, and remember.

This week, as you look at the bare tree, let it speak. It stands stripped and unadorned, just as the cross stood on Calvary. No beauty to attract us. No covering to hide behind. Just a tree — the same instrument God has used from the beginning to show us who He is, what we have lost, and what He has done to bring us home.

The readings below trace the thread of the tree through the whole Bible — from creation to the cross to the new creation. Read them together as a family. Let your children see that this tree in your home is connected to the oldest and most important story in the world.

The Thread of the Tree: Genesis to Revelation

Creation & Fall

  • Genesis 2:4–17 God plants a garden and places two trees at its center — the Tree of Life and the Tree of the Knowledge of Good and Evil
  • Genesis 3:1–24 The forbidden tree, the fall, and exile from the Tree of Life — the garden is closed

Curse & Law

  • Deuteronomy 21:22–23 "A hanged man is cursed by God" — the law that would one day point to Calvary

Wisdom & Flourishing

  • Psalm 1:1–6 The righteous like a tree planted by streams of water — the flourishing life God always intended
  • Proverbs 3:13–20 Wisdom "is a tree of life to those who lay hold of her" — blessing flows from the fear of the Lord
  • Proverbs 11:28–31 "The fruit of the righteous is a tree of life" — the one who wins souls is wise

Judgment & Promise

  • Isaiah 61:1–6 God's people called "oaks of righteousness, a planting of the Lord" — beauty for ashes
  • Ezekiel 17:22–24 God plants a tender sprig on a high mountain — all the trees of the field will know He is Lord
  • Daniel 4:10–26 Nebuchadnezzar's dream of the great tree cut down — God humbles the proud

Prophetic Imagery

  • Jeremiah 17:5–10 "Blessed is the man who trusts in the Lord... He is like a tree planted by water" — two destinies contrasted
  • Zechariah 11:1–6 The cedars and oaks fall — judgment sweeps through the proud trees of the nations

The Cross

  • Acts 5:27–33 "The God of our fathers raised Jesus, whom you killed by hanging him on a tree"
  • Acts 10:34–43 "They put him to death by hanging him on a tree" — and the promise of forgiveness through His name
  • Galatians 3:10–14 "Cursed is everyone who is hanged on a tree" — Christ bears the curse so that blessing might come
  • 1 Peter 2:21–25 "He himself bore our sins in his body on the tree" — the Shepherd who brings us home

Fruit & Discipleship

  • Matthew 7:15–23 "Every good tree bears good fruit" — you will recognize them by their fruit
  • Matthew 12:33–37 "Either make the tree good and its fruit good, or make the tree bad" — the heart revealed
  • Luke 13:6–9 The parable of the barren fig tree — patience, judgment, and one more year of grace
  • John 15:1–8 "I am the vine, you are the branches" — abiding in Christ, the living wood

Restoration

  • Revelation 2:1–7 "To the one who conquers I will grant to eat of the tree of life" — the promise renewed
  • Revelation 22:1–5 The Tree of Life restored, bearing twelve kinds of fruit, its leaves for the healing of the nations
